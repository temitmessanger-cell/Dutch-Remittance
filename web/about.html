About Dutch Remit

Dutch Remit, built by Dutch Inc, is Cameroon's biggest cross-border remittance and virtual card platform, and one of Africa's leading platforms for sending money and spending online — built for Cameroonians and the wider African diaspora, with coverage reaching Europe, the UAE and Asia, backed by global fintech infrastructure.

Where Dutch Remit reaches

Dutch Remit connects Cameroon and the wider African diaspora to 120+ countries worldwide. Mobile money and bank payouts land instantly across a confirmed set of African, European and North American destinations, and business-style wire transfers extend that reach into Europe, the United Arab Emirates, Asia and beyond — so whether you're supporting family at home or paying an overseas supplier, there's a rail built for it.

Currencies, including local money

Dutch Remit supports 20+ currencies. That includes local African money — the Central African CFA franc (XAF), Nigerian Naira (NGN), and Ghanaian Cedi (GHS) — held right alongside major world currencies like USD, EUR and GBP, and stablecoins USDC and USDT. Your wallet balance can be held in XAF, USD or USDT from day one, with NGN and GHS unlocked once you set up a local bank account in that currency.

What you can do

  • Send money to friends, family, or businesses, locally or abroad — mobile money and bank payouts across 120+ countries, with wire transfers reaching Europe, the UAE, Asia and beyond
  • Get paid with real, live exchange rates on international transfers, in 20+ currencies including local money like XAF, NGN and GHS
  • Manage your cards — add, view, and remove cards securely
  • Top up or withdraw using mobile money, bank transfer, or crypto
  • Send gifts for birthdays, holidays, and other occasions

Built on global fintech infrastructure

Every transfer, deposit, withdrawal and card runs through licensed payment partners rather than a black box — real, regulated financial rails behind a simple app. That's what lets Dutch Remit move money quickly and reliably across so many corridors at once, instead of building and maintaining a separate banking relationship in every country.
